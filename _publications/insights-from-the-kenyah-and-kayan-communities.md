excerpt: <details><summary>Abstract</summary>'Extended reality (XR) technology has demonstrated significant potential in supporting cultural preservation. However, its application in preserving the Indigenous Medicinal Knowledge (IMK) of the Kenyah and Kayan communities in Malaysia remains limited. Existing XR-related studies involving these communities have primarily focused on traditional clothing, weapons, musical instruments and tourism applications, with minimal attention given to traditional medicinal practices. To address this gap, a preliminary study was conducted involving 71 Kenyah and Kayan participants from selected rural and urban areas in Malaysia. A mixed-methods approach was employed, incorporating the System Usability Scale (SUS), Simulator Sickness Questionnaire (SSQ), observations and informal interviews to explore community perceptions of augmented reality (AR) and virtual reality (VR) technologies for IMK preservation. The prototype achieved mean SUS scores of 62.00±15.45 for AR and 53.50±9.45 for VR, indicating moderate usability. Meanwhile, SSQ findings recorded total scores of 6.73±8.11 for AR and 38.90±21.58 for VR, suggesting higher levels of discomfort in VR, particularly in relation to oculomotor effects. Despite limited prior exposure to immersive technologies, participants generally responded positively to both XR experiences, with AR often preferred due to its familiarity and accessibility. Thematic analysis further identified four key considerations for future XR-based IMK applications:- cultural relevance and identity, ease of use, technical accessibility and educational value. These findings provide preliminary insights into community acceptance of XR technologies for indigenous knowledge preservation and offer design considerations to inform the development of future XR-based IMK preservation platforms.'</details>
