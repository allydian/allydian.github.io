excerpt: <details><summary>Abstract</summary>Indigenous knowledge of traditional medicine, which has sustained Borneo''s ethnic tribes for generations, is facing extinction due to the preference for modern medicine and a lack of motivation to pass down this wisdom. To combat this potential loss, this study explored the usability and user engagement of a smartphone-based extended reality application, Herbal Heritage, a gamified learning tool for Kenyah and Kayan medicinal practices. The study employed a mixed-methods approach to identify which gamification elements would best motivate users when utilizing augmented reality (AR) and non-immersive virtual reality (VR). Data collection methods included a post-test questionnaire that used the System Usability Scale (SUS), Intrinsic Motivation Inventory (IMI), observations, and open-ended interviews. The data collection was conducted at the FORUM Cultural Centre, Miri, with the Kenyah and Kayan communities. It found that a unified device offering both gamified AR and VR experiences was well-received by the community. The final prototype was highly successful, with participants deeming the immersive, gamified elements a valuable and effective means of preserving this indigenous knowledge.</details>
